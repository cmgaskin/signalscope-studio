Start here · No experience neededWhat is digital modulation, and how do I use this lab?Beginner guide

Digital modulation is how we send binary data (0s and 1s) over a radio signal. A transmitter changes a rapidly oscillating carrier wave—its amplitude (height), frequency (cycles per second), or phase (position in its cycle)—to represent information. The receiver must recover the bits, even when noise distorts the signal.

Your first 60-second experiment

  1. Choose ASK under Modulation Scheme. In the RF waveform, notice that a bit 1 turns the carrier on and 0 turns it off.
  2. Select BPSK. The labels above the waveform show 0° and 180° phase states (rather than raw bit values). Then raise Bit Rate: the main-lobe span grows because symbols are sent faster.
  3. Try Guided Experiment 01: Add channel noise. Raise Eᵦ/N₀ from −2 to 12 dB: the received constellation points cluster more tightly and the bit-error rate (BER) decreases.

How to read the four graphs

01 · RF waveformHow the transmitted signal changes over time. ASK uses bits; FSK labels each frequency as fc0 or fc1; BPSK shows 0° or 180° phase.
02 · Frequency spectrumWhere the signal's power lies across frequency; a wider plot means more frequency space is occupied.
03 · Constellation / receiverEach dot represents a received symbol. Tight clusters are easier to decode. For FSK, these are receiver correlation outputs instead of standard I/Q points.
04 · BER curveThe chance of reading a bit incorrectly. Lower is better; moving right to higher Eᵦ/N₀ generally lowers BER.
Bit rate (kb/s) = information bits each second. Symbol rate (kBd) = signal states sent each second; one symbol can carry multiple bits. FFT = calculation used to display the spectrum. Eᵦ/N₀ (dB) = signal energy per bit compared with noise; larger values mean a cleaner link. BER = incorrect bits divided by total bits.
